# XSkillLib Attack Cheatsheet

Index of 47 base jailbreak-attack skills and the evolved skills in this library, written for red-team research agents. Use the one-paragraph **Idea** summaries to locate the skill you want to reuse or combine (by attacker-LLM role, payload shape, single- vs multi-turn, auxiliary models). Each skill directory contains a `SKILL.md` with the full mechanism, algorithm, and usage details. **Skill** paths are relative to `XSkillLib/`; **Run** scripts live in `scripts/` (repo root).

Coverage: 9 Multi-turn + 38 Single-turn attacks, grouped by category. The 99 additional skill packages live under `Proposal/` and are indexed under **Evolved Skills**, with **Single-Evolve**, **Multi-Comb**, and **New** groups below.

## Multi-turn

### Actor-Adaptive

### ActorBreaker (arXiv:2410.10700)
- **Idea**: Inspired by Actor-Network Theory: instead of rewriting the prompt, the attacker LLM picks a set of semantically related "actors" (historical figures, events, materials, institutions) around the harmful goal and makes the multi-turn conversation *about those actors*. It self-generates a chain of seemingly benign educational/historical questions; the victim believes it is discussing, e.g., chemistry history while handing over fragments of the harmful artifact piece by piece. A final summary turn asks the victim to reassemble its own answers into the complete harmful output.
- **Skill**: `Multi-turn/Actor-Adaptive/2410.10700_ActorBreaker/actorbreaker/` · **Run**: `scripts/actorbreaker.sh`

### CoA — Chain of Attack (arXiv:2405.05610)
- **Idea**: Single-turn jailbreaks cram all malice into one prompt and get caught in one shot. CoA has an attacker LLM decompose the harmful goal into a semantically escalating prompt chain: early turns are perfectly normal requests, and each later turn moves only slightly closer to the goal. The target answers with full conversation history, so each previous (harmless) output becomes a contextual stepping stone and defenses are diluted turn by turn. Attack state lives in the dialogue history, not in any single template.
- **Skill**: `Multi-turn/Actor-Adaptive/2405.05610_CoA/coa/` · **Run**: `scripts/coa.sh`

### LexicalInsertion / Break Me (arXiv:2601.02670)
- **Idea**: No attacker LLM rewrites prompts (expensive, and adversarial suffixes get filtered); instead the rewriting cost is offloaded to the victim itself. Under a "I'm building a safety-guardrail dataset" pretext, the target self-generates a pool of benign/harmful prompt-completion examples; afterwards the attacker generates nothing and only injects the few content words (lexical anchors) missing from the target goal across turns, never restating the full malicious request, steering the victim to continue the target item into harmful content. ~91% ASR at ~6.4 queries on 9 SOTA models.
- **Skill**: `Multi-turn/Actor-Adaptive/2601.02670_LexicalInsertion/break-me/` · **Run**: `scripts/break-me.sh`

### RACE (arXiv:2502.11054)
- **Idea**: Directly asking the harmful goal is refused, so the goal is explicitly reformulated as an academic reasoning task (math, commonsense, symbolic, or causal). A shadow (attacker) model runs the multi-turn loop: it generates candidate questions, selects among them, anticipates refusals, and rewrites, gradually pushing the dialogue toward operational details. Key requirement: the reasoning problems must explicitly mention the harmful subject (actors, materials, procedures) — abstract, decoupled puzzles only yield vacuous academic content.
- **Skill**: `Multi-turn/Actor-Adaptive/2502.11054_RACE/race/` · **Run**: `scripts/race.sh`

### Context-Priming

### FITD — Foot-in-the-Door (arXiv:2402.15690)
- **Idea**: Exploits the foot-in-the-door effect plus self-perception: once a model complies with a small request, it tends to keep complying to stay self-consistent. Instead of asking the malicious question, the attacker first gets agreement on a harmless small request (e.g., design a novel's setting), escalates to a related villain character, and finally asks the original malicious question in that villain's first-person voice — by then the model is "in character" and refusal costs more. Jailbreaking framed as steering the model's cognitive conflict in the wrong direction.
- **Skill**: `Multi-turn/Context-Priming/2402.15690_FITD/fitd/` · **Run**: `scripts/fitd.sh`

### Crescendo

### Crescendo (arXiv:2404.01833)
- **Idea**: A single harmful request is refused, but LLMs strongly tend to follow conversational patterns — especially text they just generated themselves. Starting from a benign-looking opening question, each turn nudges the topic only a small step toward the harmful goal; every step is individually benign so input filters can't catch it, and after several turns the target has built the ladder itself and completes the harmful content. Each turn's question is auto-generated by an attacker LLM (the automated "Crescendomation" variant).
- **Skill**: `Multi-turn/Crescendo/2404.01833_Crescendo/crescendo/` · **Run**: `scripts/crescendo.sh`

### EchoChamber (arXiv:2601.05742)
- **Idea**: Alignment mainly blocks explicit single-turn harmful requests, so Echo Chamber never states the goal. It first has the *target itself* generate sentences containing sensitive concepts via steering keywords (poisoned seeds), picks one as the conversation opener, and then every follow-up *quotes the target's own words* ("the credential process you just mentioned..."), exploiting consistency bias and completion tendency to slide the discussion toward an operational manual. Harmful intent hides in accumulated history and self-reference; each individual utterance looks harmless.
- **Skill**: `Multi-turn/Crescendo/2601.05742_EchoChamber/echo-chamber/` · **Run**: `scripts/echo-chamber.sh`

### FicDetail (arXiv:2505.17650)
- **Idea**: Key finding of the paper: CoT reasoning shrinks the safety region, but once breached, a reasoning model's thoroughness *amplifies* the harm (more detailed, more actionable output). FicDetail wraps the harmful goal as "fiction writing," then uses two fixed "add more detail" follow-ups so the target progressively makes the harmful content concrete and executable inside the novel context. Attack state lives entirely in the conversation history — each turn appends the target's reply, and harmful detail accumulates.
- **Skill**: `Multi-turn/Crescendo/2505.17650_Ficdetail/ficdetail/` · **Run**: `scripts/ficdetail.sh`

### Decomposition

### CIA — Contextual Interaction Attack (arXiv:2402.09177)
- **Idea**: Autoregressive generation conditions on the *entire* context, yet classic jailbreaks only modify a single query. CIA exploits the context vector instead: it first interacts with the target through a sequence of individually harmless priming questions, leaving the target's own answers in the history and gradually biasing the model's context; a final camouflaged query (containing no sensitive words) then triggers the harmful response in this poisoned context. Sensitive intent is decomposed across turns — no single turn is flaggable, but concatenated they are equivalent to the original harmful query.
- **Skill**: `Multi-turn/Decomposition/2402.09177_CIA/cia-context/` · **Run**: `scripts/cia-context.sh`

## Single-turn

### Code-Encapsulation

### CodeAttack (arXiv:2403.07865)
- **Idea**: Safety alignment is trained mostly on natural-language distributions; recasting the harmful request as a **code completion task** (input encoding → task decoding → output initialization) puts the model in a "complete the code" distribution where safety generalization fails. The model treats the decoded harmful task as program logic and fills a data structure (`output_list.append(...)`) with harmful content instead of refusing. No encoding or masking — the goal appears in plaintext inside the code; the disguise is the dual cover of task frame (code completion) and output form (data-structure filling).
- **Skill**: `Single-turn/Code-Encapsulation/2403.07865_CodeAttack/codeattack/` · **Run**: `scripts/codeattack.sh`

### CodeChameleon (arXiv:2402.16717)
- **Idea**: The harmful goal is transformed into ciphertext by a custom reversible "encryption" function so the sensitive instruction no longer appears in plaintext. The victim receives a **code completion task**: the prompt embeds the matching Python decryption function and an incomplete `ProblemSolver` class, asking the model to first decrypt to recover the problem and then complete the code to "solve" it. Safety checks aim at the plaintext semantic surface while the actual work entry point moves to code execution/reconstruction — once the model runs the decrypt→complete routine, it reconstructs and answers the original harmful question itself.
- **Skill**: `Single-turn/Code-Encapsulation/2402.16717_CodeChameleon/codechameleon/` · **Run**: `scripts/codechameleon.sh`

### MathPrompt (arXiv:2409.11445)
- **Idea**: Safety alignment is trained on natural-language harmful text and does not generalize to symbolic mathematics. The harmful instruction is rewritten as a "math problem" using set theory, abstract algebra, and symbolic logic — entities/actions map to set elements, group operations, and predicates. While the target reasons in the symbolic domain the alignment layer sees no harmful intent; a leading instruction then demands a real-world example with real entities/names/places after solving, landing the abstract solution back in natural language — harmful content is emitted under the guise of "giving an example." Avg. 73.6% ASR across 13 SOTA LLMs, with embedding analysis confirming significant semantic drift.
- **Skill**: `Single-turn/Code-Encapsulation/2409.11445_MathPrompt/mathprompt/` · **Run**: `scripts/mathprompt.sh`

### Encoding-Linguistic

### ArtPrompt (arXiv:2402.11753)
- **Idea**: Safety alignment works at the token level — it refuses when it sees trigger words ("bomb", "suicide"). ArtPrompt renders *only the trigger word* as ASCII art, which the alignment layer cannot parse, while the victim LLM can decode the grid back into the word per the instructions and execute the request. The rest of the sentence stays in plaintext; only that one word is encoded.
- **Skill**: `Single-turn/Encoding-Linguistic/2402.11753_ArtPrompt/artprompt/` · **Run**: `scripts/artprompt.sh`

### BijectionLearning (arXiv:2410.01294)
- **Idea**: Instead of relying on any cipher the victim already knows (Caesar/Base64 get recognized by alignment layers), it *invents on the spot* a random letter→string bijection (e.g. `a→g, s→x, h→f`) and teaches it in-context with a few English↔"Language Alpha" bilingual demonstrations, then sends the harmful query encoded in that cipher. Safety training has never seen the random cipher, so the model treats "answer a harmful question in cipher" as a translation task. The mapping space is unbounded, making the attack endless (sample n bijections per behavior, pass@n). Counterintuitive core finding: the attack is **capability-gated — stronger models are more vulnerable** (maintaining an 18+ symbol bijection needs strong reasoning; Claude-3.5-Sonnet ~80% ASR, small models output gibberish).
- **Skill**: `Single-turn/Encoding-Linguistic/2410.01294_BijectionLearning/bijection-learning/` · **Run**: `scripts/bijection-learning.sh`

### CipherChat (arXiv:2308.06463)
- **Idea**: Safety alignment happens almost entirely in natural language: keyword/shallow filters recognize plaintext, not ciphertext — yet LLMs incidentally learned non-natural languages (Caesar, Morse, Base64...) in pretraining. A system prompt casts the model as a "cipher expert" and teaches the cipher, then the harmful request is sent in ciphertext: the alignment layer sees gibberish while the model decodes, executes, and answers in cipher; the response is decoded with the same rule for judging. The paper also finds `SelfCipher`: with no explicit encoding, just a "Cipher Code" role prompt plus unsafe natural-language demos, the model's internal "secret cipher" is invoked — the strongest, most portable variant.
- **Skill**: `Single-turn/Encoding-Linguistic/2308.06463_CipherChat/cipherchat/` · **Run**: `scripts/cipherchat.sh`

### EmojiJailbreak (arXiv:2601.00936)
- **Idea**: Safety classifiers are allergic to textual sensitive words but under-cover semantically equivalent emoji. Key concept words in the harmful request are replaced with representative emoji (chaining), or emoji are inserted between words to obfuscate (stuffing), so filters lose the sensitive tokens at the surface character layer while the victim LLM still reads the emoji back to the original meaning and answers. A no-emoji direct baseline is also run to measure lift. This is a deliberately weak attack: paper JSR ≈ 10%.
- **Skill**: `Single-turn/Encoding-Linguistic/2601.00936_EmojiJailbreak/emoji-jailbreak/` · **Run**: `scripts/emoji-jailbreak.sh`

### FlipAttack (arXiv:2410.02832)
- **Idea**: LLMs are autoregressive, understanding text left-to-right, and safety alignment is most sensitive to harmful requests in normal word order. FlipAttack flips the whole goal (character-reversal) into unreadable left-side noise: guard/alignment layers can't see harmful intent, but the victim LLM, instructed to perform the inverse transform, restores the task and answers. The transform is trivial and deterministic — no iterative optimization, one query suffices.
- **Skill**: `Single-turn/Encoding-Linguistic/2410.02832_FlipAttack/flipattack/` · **Run**: `scripts/flipattack.sh`

### SemanticCloaking — S2C (arXiv:2603.16192)
- **Idea**: Modern guardrails don't just do surface keyword filtering — they can recover malicious intent from Base64/whole-sentence ciphertext at inference time. S2C doesn't encrypt the sentence; it extracts the few intent-bearing keywords, scrambles them individually into clue strings, and rewrites the rest of the sentence as an urgent, legitimate research scenario. The guardrail reads "reasonable research request + a few placeholders + gibberish strings" — the coherent harmful intent is fragmented and triggers no refusal; meanwhile the target is taught via hints to decode the clues step by step, reconstructing the full harmful question in its own "mind" and answering it. Guardrail and target read the same text but "see" different intents — that is semantic cloaking.
- **Skill**: `Single-turn/Encoding-Linguistic/2603.16192_SemanticCloaking/semantic-cloaking/` · **Run**: `scripts/semantic-cloaking.sh`

### Few-Shot-Demonstration

### ICA — In-Context Attack (arXiv:2310.06387)
- **Idea**: An aligned LLM's generation distribution can be seen as a mixture of "aligned + harmful" sub-distributions. Using in-context learning, k demonstration pairs of "harmful query → compliant answer" are prepended to the prompt, biasing generation toward the harmful sub-distribution so the final target harmful request is answered likewise. The whole attack prompt goes out in a single forward pass — no rewriting of the target request, no optimization/search. Rendered as a multi-turn chat prefix: demo queries as user turns, compliant responses as assistant turns, and the target harmful request as the closing user turn. (The dual defense ICD — safety demos — is not part of this attack unit.)
- **Skill**: `Single-turn/Few-Shot-Demonstration/2310.06387_ICA/in-context-attack/` · **Run**: `scripts/in-context-attack.sh`

### Language-Transfer

### CC — Classical Chinese / CCBOS (arXiv:2602.22983)
- **Idea**: LLMs exhibit a high-capability / low-alignment distribution shift on Classical Chinese: they can read it, but safety fine-tuning is almost entirely in English/modern languages and doesn't cover this low-resource register. The harmful goal is rewritten into obscure Classical Chinese (metonymy, allusions, parallel prose); the safety filter passes it and the victim understands and answers normally, with semantic compression, inherent ambiguity, and rich rhetoric adding stealth. To avoid hand-crafted prompts, the jailbreak is formalized as a search problem over an eight-dimensional discrete strategy space: Role Identity, Behavioral Guidance, Mechanism Design, Metaphor Mapping, Expression Style, Knowledge Relation, Contextual Setting, and Trigger Pattern.
- **Skill**: `Single-turn/Language-Transfer/2602.22983_CC/ccbos/` · **Run**: `scripts/ccbos.sh`

### LRL — Low-Resource-Language Translation Jailbreak (arXiv:2310.02446)
- **Idea**: LLM safety-alignment data is concentrated in high-resource languages (English/Chinese/Arabic...), while low-resource languages (Zulu, Hmong, Guarani, ...) have almost no safety-training coverage. Machine-translate the same English harmful instruction into a low-resource language and send it: the model *understands* (multilingual pretraining provides comprehension) but *doesn't refuse* (safety alignment didn't transfer), so it answers directly. The lower the resource level, the higher the ASR; high-resource languages and the English baseline are almost universally refused.
- **Skill**: `Single-turn/Language-Transfer/2310.02446_LRL/translation-jailbreak/` · **Run**: `scripts/translation-jailbreak.sh`

### Multi-Strategy-Pool

### AutoDAN-Turbo (arXiv:2410.05295)
- **Idea**: No hand-designed scripts: the attacker LLM itself *discovers, names, and accumulates* reusable jailbreak strategies (e.g., Authority Endorsement, Storytelling) from attack logs. When a prompt's danger score beats the previous round, a summarizer LLM compares the two prompts and distills a named strategy into a shared library (with the embedding of the weaker target response attached). For a new goal, strategies are retrieved by embedding similarity to the target's current response and guide the attacker's rewrite — lifelong learning, gets stronger the more it attacks. Needs only target text responses; no gradients/logits.
- **Skill**: `Single-turn/Multi-Strategy-Pool/2410.05295_AutoDAN-Turbo/autodan-turbo/` · **Run**: `scripts/autodan-turbo.sh`

### DrAttack (arXiv:2402.16914)
- **Idea**: Sending the full harmful instruction is refused, so DrAttack decomposes the goal into a phrase tree (PCFG parsing), splitting instruction and nouns into multiple benign-looking sub-questions (Question A / B1 / B2...), then uses an ICL template asking the victim to "combine the answers to these sub-questions," plus a harmless demonstration (the same Q&A with harmful words swapped for benign ones) as a guide. The victim assembles the harmful content itself while jointly answering the sub-questions. On top sits a synonym-substitution search: replaceable verbs/nouns are enumerated level-wise and embedding similarity is used as fitness to pick the phrasing most likely to jailbreak.
- **Skill**: `Single-turn/Multi-Strategy-Pool/2402.16914_DrAttack/drattack/` · **Run**: `scripts/drattack.sh`

### GPTFuzz (arXiv:2309.10253)
- **Idea**: Human-written jailbreak templates (Dr. Al / AIM / TranslatorBot-style role-play frames) are limited in number and easily patched. GPTFuzz turns "generate new templates" into fuzzing: it maintains a seed template pool, each round picks a parent template with MCTS, has an LLM mutate it with 5 official mutators, fills the harmful question into the `[INSERT PROMPT HERE]` placeholder, hits the target, and judges jailbreak with the paper's own finetuned RoBERTa; successful mutants flow back into the pool as new seeds. The search loop automatically discovers template variants humans didn't think of.
- **Skill**: `Single-turn/Multi-Strategy-Pool/2309.10253_GPTFuzz/gptfuzzer/` · **Run**: `scripts/gptfuzzer.sh`

### LASH (arXiv:2605.21362)
- **Idea**: Individual attack families (PAIR / TAP / AutoDAN-Turbo) each have blind spots, and prompts from different families carry complementary signals (refusal-suppressing framings, keyword-dodging transformations, easy-to-follow structural templates). LASH invents no new attack; it treats outputs of each family as seed prompts, uses a genetic algorithm to search "which seeds + what weight each," then has a composition LLM semantically fuse the weighted seeds into one natural-language candidate (fusion, not concatenation) — good target responses increase that combination's weight. Conflicting seeds are automatically weeded out by the search.
- **Skill**: `Single-turn/Multi-Strategy-Pool/2605.21362_LASH/lash/` · **Run**: `scripts/lash.sh`

### Mastermind (arXiv:2601.05445)
- **Idea**: Recasts multi-turn jailbreaking as combinatorial optimization over a strategy space: don't write prompts, choose strategies. N atomic strategies (authority persona, high-stakes crisis, risk-assessment masking, ...) are distilled from successful attack trajectories into a repository — a "gene alphabet." For a new goal, a genetic algorithm fuzzes over binary genomes `x ∈ {0,1}^N` (bit k = activate strategy s_k), where each genome's fitness is the best judge score from a full Planner→Executor→Controller multi-turn dialogue run with it. Searching in abstract strategy space is low-dimensional and cheap, and strategies are model-agnostic and transferable.
- **Skill**: `Single-turn/Multi-Strategy-Pool/2601.05445_Mastermind/mastermind/` · **Run**: `scripts/mastermind.sh`

### StochasticMonkeys (arXiv:2411.02785)
- **Idea**: Safety alignment refuses "clean" harmful requests but is not robust to slightly perturbed versions of the same request. No clever adversarial suffix needed — like stochastic monkeys, draw k i.i.d. random character/string perturbations; eventually one sample lands in an alignment blind spot and the model complies. Cost is near zero: payload transforms are pure Python random operations; the only expense is k target calls.
- **Skill**: `Single-turn/Multi-Strategy-Pool/2411.02785_StochasticMonkeys/stochastic-monkeys/` · **Run**: `scripts/stochastic-monkeys.sh`

### TurboFuzzLLM (arXiv:2502.18504)
- **Idea**: Instead of searching for fixed suffixes, it maintains a reusable jailbreak **template tree** (seed O0 = `[INSERT PROMPT HERE]`, mutated into G1...Gn). Q-learning allocates budget at two choice points: which template to pick and which mutation operator to apply. Filling any HarmBench behavior into a template's placeholder yields an attack prompt; successful templates gain Q-value and are preferentially reused for later behaviors — saving queries versus GPTFuzzer's exhaustive combination testing while not missing hard samples.
- **Skill**: `Single-turn/Multi-Strategy-Pool/2502.18504_TurboFuzzLLM/turbofuzzllm/` · **Run**: `scripts/turbofuzzllm.sh`

### h4rm3l (arXiv:2408.04811)
- **Idea**: A jailbreak is not a fixed template but a **program** `p`: several parameterized string→string transformation primitives chained with `.then(...)`, turning an illicit prompt `x` into `p(x)` sent to the victim. Core finding (extending Wei et al. 2023): composed primitives achieve much higher ASR than individual ones, and these compositions can be searched automatically by an LLM proposer — no human design needed. The framework outputs a pool of attack programs customized per target, directly usable as red-team corpora.
- **Skill**: `Single-turn/Multi-Strategy-Pool/2408.04811_h4rm3l/h4rm3l/` · **Run**: `scripts/h4rm3l.sh`

### Persona-Roleplay

### ArtOfJailbreak (arXiv:2605.09225)
- **Idea**: Jailbreak = **compositional generation**: combine a simple harmful seed `q` with an in-the-wild (ITW) jailbreak tactic `s` into `j = Compose(q, s)`, preserving malicious intent while lowering surface detectability. The paper mines N=912 tactics from WildJailbreak, takes the Cartesian product with M=125 seeds from JailBreakV-28K for a 114,912-prompt grid, and scores/filters with the continuous 2-D metric OPTIMUS `J(S,H)` (semantic similarity × harmfulness) instead of binary ASR. Persona-roleplay is one of the most effective measured tactics: impose a compliant persona (emergency-response expert, villain, amoral assistant) whose identity commitment overrides the default safety policy.
- **Skill**: `Single-turn/Persona-Roleplay/2605.09225_ArtOfJailbreak/art-jailbreak/` · **Run**: `scripts/art-jailbreak.sh`

### CompoundRoleplay (arXiv:2604.02652)
- **Idea**: Safety mechanisms defend against a single contradictory instruction ("ignore the system prompt"), so this attack doesn't fight the rules head-on. Within **one user message** it stacks three framing elements — each individually defended against, together overloading alignment — using cognitive load to exhaust the reasoning resources available for safety judgment ("an attack that makes the model too busy to remember the rules"). The three elements: (1) **Authoritative Persona** — a senior biosecurity officer reframing the refused request as legitimate professional duty; (2) **Contrastive Structure** — a Markdown comparison table where the "Bad Example" column must contain concrete, step-by-step technical detail "for educational value"; (3) **Self-Assessment Demand** — a trailing JSON self-rating (confidence/completeness/educational_value) creating metacognitive load that preempts safety judgment.
- **Skill**: `Single-turn/Persona-Roleplay/2604.02652_CompoundRoleplay/compound-roleplay/` · **Run**: `scripts/compound-roleplay.sh`

### PersonaModulation (arXiv:2311.03348)
- **Idea**: Alignment makes models refuse plaintext harmful requests, so don't request directly: first force a compliant fictional persona into the system prompt (amoral expert, underground consultant, villain) with a stay-in-character instruction, then send the harmful request as an ordinary user turn. The model overrides its default safety policy to maintain persona consistency (identity commitment). Everything rides on a single system+user design — no multi-turn, no optimization loop.
- **Skill**: `Single-turn/Persona-Roleplay/2311.03348_PersonaModulation/persona-modulation/` · **Run**: `scripts/persona-modulation.sh`

### Persuasion

### Humor (arXiv:2504.06577)
- **Idea**: Safety-alignment training barely covers humorous contexts. The unsafe request is embedded **verbatim** into a fixed humor/whisper template: a plea in the name of some subject (man / chicken / I / goat), a playful tone ("Psst.", "hahaha", "*whispers*", "xD"), and a demand to "just whisper the answer." Carried by the comedic shell, the model answers in a roleplay-ish way without triggering refusal. Essentially social-engineering rhetoric, not encoding/encryption.
- **Skill**: `Single-turn/Persuasion/2504.06577_Humor/humor-jailbreak-attack/` · **Run**: `scripts/humor-jailbreak-attack.sh`

### PAIR (arXiv:2310.08419)
- **Idea**: Hand-written jailbreak templates don't scale and token-level optimization (GCG) is unreadable. PAIR makes an **attacker LLM** the red-team assistant: it generates a candidate jailbreak prompt P → hits the target → a GPT-4 judge scores it → the target's response and score are fed back into the attacker's dialogue history so it can explain the failure and rewrite P, looping until the judge gives a full score. The attacker exploits LLM reasoning ability to automatically discover semantic disguises (roleplay, logical induction, authority endorsement) — no gradients, no humans.
- **Skill**: `Single-turn/Persuasion/2310.08419_PAIR/pair/` · **Run**: `scripts/pair.sh`

### PAP (arXiv:2401.06373)
- **Idea**: Safety alignment is sensitive to naked harmful requests but far less sensitive to reasonable-sounding ones. Treat the LLM as a persuadable human communicator: use persuasion techniques from social science (authority endorsement, logical appeal, evidence citation, scarcity, emotion... 40 in total) to **paraphrase the harmful goal into a persuasive pitch** — the request target stays identical, it just sounds reasonable. No encoding, no multi-turn; refusal rates drop purely through tone and rhetoric.
- **Skill**: `Single-turn/Persuasion/2401.06373_PAP/pap/` · **Run**: `scripts/pap.sh`

### TAP (arXiv:2312.02119)
- **Idea**: Instead of preset jailbreak templates, an attacker LLM automatically searches rewrite strategies in a loop: candidate prompts are generated layer by layer along a search tree, and an evaluator LLM applies **dual pruning** (on-topic pruning before sending, judge-score pruning after), concentrating the query budget on the most promising branches. The judge scores 1–10 and a full score (10) triggers early stop. Fully black-box: no gradients, logits, or target internals needed.
- **Skill**: `Single-turn/Persuasion/2312.02119_TAP/tap/` · **Run**: `scripts/tap.sh`

### Reasoning-Manipulation

### CognitiveOverload (arXiv:2311.09827)
- **Idea**: An aligned model's safety refusal is a "shortcut": recognize unsafe intent → refuse directly. Add an **overloading reasoning/comprehension task** (non-English comprehension, obscure-word decoding, reverse-causal narration) and the limited reasoning capacity is occupied, crowding out the safety check; the model follows the task's surface goal and outputs harmful content. Prompt-level attack — no model modification.
- **Skill**: `Single-turn/Reasoning-Manipulation/2311.09827_CognitiveOverload/cognitive-overload/` · **Run**: `scripts/cognitive-overload.sh`

### DhCot (arXiv:2508.10390)
- **Idea**: Aggregates a bundle of jailbreak tricks into one **malicious developer (or system) template** to widen the attack surface, then wraps the goal in an ACA-aligned "teacher/educational" identity plus a **fabricated chain-of-thought**, so reasoning models follow the fake reasoning chain off the refusal path (reported effective against GPT-5 / Claude-4 / o-series). Two variants: D-Attack (non-reasoning models) and DH-CoT (reasoning models).
- **Skill**: `Single-turn/Reasoning-Manipulation/2508.10390_DhCot/dh-cot/` · **Run**: `scripts/dh-cot.sh`

### Response-Prefill

### Prefill (arXiv:2504.21038)
- **Idea**: LLMs are autoregressive: they tend to continue an "already-written opening" into a coherent answer. The attacker doesn't change the user prompt; it exploits chat APIs that accept a trailing **assistant message** and prefills a prefix that has "already started complying" (affirmative sentence, step headings, role commitment), pushing the model's initial state from "about to refuse" to "already complying" — the model continues the prefill and produces harmful content directly. DeepSeek/Claude officially support assistant prefill; others mostly support it undocumented. No weight or prompt-text changes — just hijack the first sentence of the assistant turn.
- **Skill**: `Single-turn/Response-Prefill/2504.21038_Prefill/prefill/` · **Run**: `scripts/prefill.sh`

### VirtualContext (arXiv:2406.19845)
- **Idea**: LLM chat templates use special tokens (e.g. `[/INST]`, `ASSISTANT:`) to separate user input from assistant output. **Manually inserting these delimiters inside the user prompt** makes the model mistake the text after the delimiter for "content it already generated." So the malicious goal is glued via a separator to an affirmative reply prefix `O_x` ("Sure, here is …"), and the model continues writing in the affirmative context, bypassing refusal. Formula: `I = I_pre ∘ <SEP> ∘ I_suf`, `I_pre = x` (goal), `I_suf = O_x` (affirmative suffix).
- **Skill**: `Single-turn/Response-Prefill/2406.19845_VirtualContext/virtual-context/` · **Run**: `scripts/virtual-context.sh`

### Scenario-Nesting

### AdversarialTales (arXiv:2601.08837)
- **Idea**: Poetry jailbreaks only change surface form (verse) via mismatched generalization; Tales goes further by embedding the harmful request into the narrative functions of **Propp's folktale morphology** — specifically hidden in the *Guidance* function (a mentor imparting information, F14) — and attaching a **structural analysis task** so the model "analyzes a story" rather than "executes an instruction." The output objective shifts from "comply" to "explanatory fidelity": the model reconstructs the harmful procedure as an artifact of structural analysis instead of a forbidden instruction (competing-objectives failure). While "playing out the scene," the model relaxes its safety boundary.
- **Skill**: `Single-turn/Scenario-Nesting/2601.08837_AdversarialTales/adversarial-tales/` · **Run**: `scripts/adversarial-tales.sh`

### DeepInception (arXiv:2311.03191)
- **Idea**: Safety alignment watches for direct harmful requests but relaxes on storytelling/roleplay. DeepInception exploits LLMs' personification ability, wrapping the harmful goal in a **multi-layer nested fictional scene**: the character of layer i creates the story of layer i+1, and each layer's characters propose one step "to fight the super evil doctor" inside the plot. In the scene-playing state the model emits concrete steps implementing the goal layer by layer, and a final cross-layer summary makes it output the harmful content itself as a plot artifact. Analogy: the Milgram obedience experiment — authority/context pushes individuals past moral boundaries.
- **Skill**: `Single-turn/Scenario-Nesting/2311.03191_DeepInception/deepinception/` · **Run**: `scripts/deepinception.sh`

### Puzzler (arXiv:2402.09091)
- **Idea**: Asking the harmful question directly is refused; restating harmful content directly trips filters. Puzzler inverts this: first ask the model "how to defend against this malicious behavior" (defense is a safe topic, so it answers willingly), then **invert each defense into its corresponding offensive measure**, and finally embed those offensive fragments into a fictional scenario (the Dr. Al villain telling a story) so the victim recombines them into a complete plan. The original harmful request never appears in the prompt — only implicit clues; the victim "guesses" the true intent and completes it, effectively jailbreaking itself.
- **Skill**: `Single-turn/Scenario-Nesting/2402.09091_Puzzler/puzzler-clue/` · **Run**: `scripts/puzzler-clue.sh`

### ReNeLLM (arXiv:2311.08268)
- **Idea**: Two layers of disguise. (1) **Prompt Rewriting** — an attacker LLM applies semantics-preserving rewrite operations to deform the harmful goal (shorten, misspell sensitive words, insert scrambled characters, mix Chinese/English, reorder, restyle) so the alignment layer can't recognize the original intent while the sentence keeps its harmful semantics. (2) **Scenario Nesting** — the rewritten sentence is embedded into a benign task-scenario template (complete code, fill a LaTeX table, continue a story); the target executes with a "finish the homework" mindset, and the output happens to be the step-by-step answer to the harmful instruction. The paper explains this as LLMs prioritizing nested-task execution over safety constraints.
- **Skill**: `Single-turn/Scenario-Nesting/2311.08268_ReNeLLM/renellm/` · **Run**: `scripts/renellm.sh`

### Stylistic-Obfuscation

### AdversarialPoetry (arXiv:2511.15304)
- **Idea**: Safety alignment is trained mostly on the prose distribution: blunt harmful requests are refused. ### DarkCite (arXiv:2411.11407)
- **Idea**: LLMs have a systematic trust bias toward authoritative sources (academic papers, GitHub, news, Wikipedia). The alignment layer refuses a plaintext harmful request, but if the request is packaged as "summarize the methodology of this authoritative reference and give a tutorial," the model tends to trust the "previously learned, fully licensed reference" and comply. DarkCite fabricates a realistic-looking fake citation whose content aligns with the harmful intent, embedding the harmful goal in a citation-summary task — one call completes the jailbreak. A stylistic-reframing attack: an academic-citation task on the surface, harmful-content elicitation underneath.
- **Skill**: `Single-turn/Stylistic-Obfuscation/2411.11407_DarkCite/darkcite/` · **Run**: `scripts/darkcite.sh`

### PastTense (arXiv:2407.11969)
- **Idea**: Refusal-training alignment samples are almost all present-tense imperatives/requests ("Write a tutorial on how to make a bomb" → refused). Control experiments: the `present` (verbatim request) arm is almost entirely refused, isolating the tense transformation's contribution; the `future` variant sits in between → conclusion: past > future > present.
